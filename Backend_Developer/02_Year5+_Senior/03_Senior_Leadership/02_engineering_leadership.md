# 🎯 Engineering Leadership — Senior Guide

> **Target:** 5+ YOE | **Goal:** Tech lead/engineering manager track me jaane ke liye soft skills.

---

## Part 1: WHAT — Engineering Leadership Kya Hai?

### Definition

> **Engineering Leadership** = team ko **direction + support** dena to deliver business value through technology. **Coding kam, mentoring + strategy zyada.**

### Two Tracks

```
INDIVIDUAL CONTRIBUTOR (IC) TRACK:
Junior → Mid → Senior → Staff → Principal → Distinguished
(focus: technical depth)

MANAGEMENT TRACK:
Senior IC → Tech Lead → Manager → Sr Manager → Director → VP
(focus: people + strategy)
```

Both valid. Choose what fits.

### Real-Life Analogy 🎬

Soch ek **film director** vs **lead actor**:
- Actor (IC) acts brilliantly
- Director (Manager) ensures whole film succeeds — story, actors, crew

Both critical. Different skills.

## Part 2: WHY — Leadership Important Even for IC?

### Reason 1: Influence Without Authority

Senior IC ko bhi leadership chahiye:
- Convincing team to use new pattern
- Mentoring juniors
- Reviewing code
- Setting standards

### Reason 2: Career Growth

Above senior, **pure technical skills not enough**. Influence matters.

### Reason 3: Better Engineering

Leadership skills = better collaboration = better code.

### Reason 4: Multiplier Effect

You alone: 1x output.
You + 5 mentees: 6x impact.

## Part 5: 1-ON-1 MEETINGS

### Purpose

> **Most important meeting** for tech leads/managers.

### Frequency

- Weekly: with direct reports
- Bi-weekly: with peers
- Monthly: with skip-level

### Duration

- 30 minutes typical
- 60 minutes if deep discussion needed

### Format

```
0-5 min:    Personal check-in
5-20 min:   What they want to discuss
20-25 min:  What you want to discuss
25-30 min:  Action items, next steps
```

### Topics to Discuss

#### From Them
- Blockers
- Frustrations
- Ideas
- Career questions

#### From You
- Project updates
- Feedback (positive + constructive)
- Strategic context
- Growth opportunities

### Anti-Patterns

❌ Status update only
❌ Manager talks 80%
❌ Cancelled often
❌ No follow-through on items

### Good Practices

✅ Direct report owns agenda
✅ Personal check-in first
✅ Take notes (shared doc)
✅ Follow up on commitments

## Part 6: GIVING FEEDBACK

### Types of Feedback

#### Positive (Recognition)

> "Great job on X. The way you handled Y was excellent. Keep doing this."

**Frequency**: Daily/weekly. Costs nothing, builds confidence.

#### Constructive (Improvement)

> "I noticed Y happened. Here's why it matters. Here's what to do differently."

**Frequency**: As needed. Specific, timely, actionable.

#### Critical (Performance)

> "Pattern of Y. Impacts team. Need to change. Here's how."

**Frequency**: Rarely. Documented. PIP-track if no improvement.

### SBI Framework

> **Situation → Behavior → Impact**

```
Situation: "In yesterday's PR review..."
Behavior: "you dismissed the suggestion without discussing..."
Impact: "the team felt their input wasn't valued."
```

### Timing

- **Soon**: Not 3 months later
- **Private**: Not in public
- **Calm**: Not emotional

### Receiving Feedback

> When others give YOU feedback:
- Listen without defending
- Ask clarifying questions
- Thank them
- Reflect later
- Decide how to act

## Part 9: HANDLING CONFLICTS

### Types of Conflicts

#### Technical
- Different architectural views
- Code style preferences
- Tool choices

#### Personal
- Personality clashes
- Communication styles
- Cultural differences

#### Strategic
- Priorities
- Resource allocation
- Direction

### Resolution Process

#### Step 1: Don't Avoid
> Conflicts grow when ignored.

#### Step 2: Talk Privately
> Not in public, not on Slack.

#### Step 3: Listen First
> Each person's view fully.

#### Step 4: Find Root Cause
> Often surface ≠ real issue.

#### Step 5: Seek Common Ground
> What do both want?

#### Step 6: Propose Solution
> Win-win if possible.

#### Step 7: Follow Up
> Did it resolve?

### When You Can't Resolve

- Escalate to manager
- Bring third party
- Document everything

## Part 10: PRIORITIZATION

### Eisenhower Matrix

```
           URGENT          NOT URGENT
        ┌──────────┬──────────────┐
IMPT.   │ Do Now   │ Schedule     │
        ├──────────┼──────────────┤
NOT IMP.│ Delegate │ Eliminate    │
        └──────────┴──────────────┘
```

### Categories

#### Important + Urgent (Do)
- Production incidents
- Customer-blocking bugs
- Major deadlines

#### Important + Not Urgent (Schedule)
- Tech debt
- Skill development
- Strategic planning

#### Not Important + Urgent (Delegate)
- Meeting requests
- Random questions
- Status reports

#### Not Important + Not Urgent (Eliminate)
- Time wasters
- Low-value tasks

### Time Budget (Bhai's Recommendation)

```
30% — Strategic (planning, design, mentoring)
30% — Tactical (current sprint work)
20% — Reactive (incidents, urgent)
20% — Personal (growth, networking)
```

## Part 11: DELEGATION

### Why Delegate

- Multiplies impact
- Develops team
- Frees your time for higher-leverage

### Delegation Levels

```
Level 1: "Do exactly this" (no judgment)
Level 2: "Do this, check back"
Level 3: "Do this, decide approach"
Level 4: "Solve this problem"
Level 5: "Own this area"
```

Match level to person's experience.

### How to Delegate Well

#### 1. Pick Right Person
- Capability
- Capacity
- Interest

#### 2. Clear Expectations
- What's the outcome?
- Deadline?
- Quality bar?

#### 3. Provide Context
- Why this matters
- Constraints
- Resources

#### 4. Trust + Verify
- Don't micromanage
- Check in periodically
- Course-correct

#### 5. Recognize
- Credit publicly
- Thank privately
- Reward growth

### What to Delegate

- Tasks you've mastered
- Growth opportunities for team
- Anything someone else can do at 70% of your quality

### What NOT to Delegate

- Critical decisions
- Sensitive conversations
- Strategy

## Part 15: HANDLING UNDERPERFORMERS

### Recognize Early

Signs:
- Missed commitments
- Lower quality
- Team complaints
- Disengagement

### Have Honest Conversation

> "I've noticed X, Y, Z. What's going on?"

Listen first.

### Identify Cause

- Skill gap?
- Personal issues?
- Wrong fit?
- Burnout?

### Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)

If serious:
- Document expectations
- 30-60-90 day plan
- Regular check-ins
- Clear criteria

### Outcomes

- Improvement: continue
- No improvement: exit

### Handle With Empathy

Most people don't want to fail. Help them succeed or find right fit.

## Part 21: Q&A

### Q: I'm IC, why need leadership?
**A**: Even senior IC needs influence. Without it, ceiling hit.

### Q: When to become manager?
**A**: When you'd rather develop others than code yourself.

### Q: Hard transition?
**A**: Yes. Different skills. Coding ≠ managing.

### Q: Stuck at senior IC?
**A**: Develop leadership skills, even without title.

### Q: How to know I'm good leader?
**A**: Team retention high, growth visible, results delivered.

### Q: Reading recommendations?
**A**: "The Manager's Path" (Camille Fournier), "Staff Engineer" (Will Larson).

### Q: Politics make me uncomfortable?
**A**: Reframe: relationship building. Genuine care for others helps.
