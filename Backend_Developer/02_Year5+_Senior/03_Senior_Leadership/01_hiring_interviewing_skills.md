# 👥 Hiring & Interviewing Skills — Senior Engineer Guide

> **Target:** 5+ YOE | **Goal:** Interview lena, hiring decision karna, signal-recognize karna. Senior responsibility.

---

## Part 1: WHAT — Senior Engineer Ka Hiring Role?

### Definition

> Senior engineer banne ke baad **tu interview leta hai** — naye engineers hire karna teri zimmedari ban jaati. Junior side se senior side me transition.

### Why You're Asked to Interview

- You understand technical bar
- You know what makes a good engineer
- You're invested in team quality
- You're a culture carrier

### Real-Life Analogy 🏫

Soch school me **senior students panel** banaate hai naye admission ke liye. Senior students decide karte hai kaun fit hai. **Tu wahi senior banaya gaya.**

---

## Part 2: WHY — Hiring Critical Kyu?

### Reason 1: One Bad Hire Costs 6 Months

> **Bad hire → 3 months figure out → 2 months PIP → 1 month exit → Team morale low → New hire process.**

Total: 6 months + team productivity loss.

### Reason 2: Good Hire Multiplies Team

10x engineer in team = whole team's output up. Worth investment.

### Reason 3: Bar Setting

You set the bar. Hire below bar → bar drops → quality drops over years.

### Reason 4: Diversity

Different perspectives = better products. Bias-free hiring critical.

### Reason 5: Career Visibility

Hiring well = signal to leadership. Promotion path.

## Part 3: HOW — Interview Architecture

### Typical Process

```
RESUME SCREEN
   ↓
RECRUITER CALL
   ↓
PHONE SCREEN (Tech)
   ↓
TAKE-HOME / CODING ASSESSMENT
   ↓
ON-SITE INTERVIEWS:
- Coding (1-2 rounds)
- System Design (1 round)
- Behavioral (1 round)
- Leadership (1 round, senior+)
- Bar Raiser (1 round)
   ↓
DEBRIEF
   ↓
HIRE / NO HIRE / NEXT LEVEL
```

## Part 6: CODING INTERVIEW — Best Practices

### Choose Right Problem

**Good problem characteristics:**
- Multiple approaches possible
- Tests algorithm + code quality
- 30-45 min completable
- Not too hard, not too easy
- No esoteric knowledge

**Bad problems:**
- Trick questions
- Memorization
- Single algorithm
- Too easy / too hard

### Structure

```
0-5 min:   Introductions, problem explained
5-10 min:  Candidate clarifies, examples
10-15 min: Approach discussion
15-35 min: Coding
35-40 min: Testing, edge cases
40-45 min: Follow-up + candidate Q&A
```

### What to Observe

#### Problem Understanding
- Asks clarifying questions?
- Gives examples?
- Catches ambiguity?

#### Problem Solving
- Multiple approaches?
- Trade-offs discussed?
- Picks reasonable approach?

#### Coding
- Clean code?
- Variable names?
- Functions structured?
- Comments where needed?

#### Communication
- Talks while coding?
- Explains thought process?
- Handles silence?

#### Testing
- Tests own code?
- Edge cases considered?
- Bugs caught?

### Signal vs Noise

**Good Signal**:
- "Let me think about edge cases first..."
- "We could use X, but Y is better because..."
- "Let me test my code..."

**Noise (Ignore)**:
- Slow typing
- Different language
- Different style

### Red Flags

🚩 Can't write any code
🚩 Doesn't communicate
🚩 Defensive about feedback
🚩 Memorized solution (just typing)
🚩 Gives up easily

### Green Flags

✅ Clear communication
✅ Asks questions
✅ Multiple approaches
✅ Self-corrects bugs
✅ Open to hints

## Part 7: SYSTEM DESIGN — Best Practices

### Choose Right Problem

**Good problems:**
- "Design Twitter"
- "Design URL shortener"
- "Design ride-sharing"
- "Design payment system"

### Structure

```
0-5 min:   Problem statement
5-10 min:  Requirements clarification
10-15 min: Capacity estimation
15-25 min: High-level design
25-35 min: Deep dive on 1-2 components
35-45 min: Trade-offs, scaling, edge cases
45-50 min: Candidate Q&A
```

### What to Test

#### Requirements
- Asks for clarification?
- Functional + non-functional?
- Defines scope?

#### Estimation
- Throughput? Storage?
- Reasonable assumptions?
- Math correct?

#### Design
- Logical architecture?
- Component justification?
- Data model?

#### Trade-offs
- Discusses alternatives?
- Justifies choices?
- Aware of trade-offs?

#### Scaling
- Bottlenecks identified?
- Solutions proposed?
- Realistic at scale?

### Levels

#### L4 (Junior+)
- Build single service
- Database choice with reason
- Basic scaling

#### L5 (Senior)
- Multi-service architecture
- Caching strategies
- Async processing
- Sharding/replication

#### L6 (Staff)
- Distributed systems theory
- CAP/PACELC understanding
- Trade-offs at extreme scale
- Geographically distributed

## Part 8: BEHAVIORAL INTERVIEW

### Star Method

> Structured behavioral questions: **STAR** = Situation, Task, Action, Result.

### Ask Open-Ended

❌ "Are you a team player?"
✅ "Tell me about a time you had conflict with a teammate."

### Common Behavioral Questions

```
1. Tell me about a time you failed.
2. Describe a difficult technical decision.
3. Tell me about a conflict with manager.
4. How did you handle tight deadline?
5. Describe a time you mentored someone.
6. Tell me about your biggest impact project.
7. Time you disagreed with team.
8. Difficult bug you debugged.
```

### Probe Deeper

Don't accept surface answers. Probe:
- "What was your specific role?"
- "What did you learn?"
- "What would you do differently?"
- "How did you measure success?"

### Listen for

- **Specifics** (not generalities)
- **Their actions** (not team's)
- **Lessons learned**
- **Honesty about failures**

### Red Flags

🚩 "I" never used — always "we"
🚩 Blames others
🚩 No failures admitted
🚩 Vague answers
🚩 Negative about past colleagues

### Green Flags

✅ Specific examples
✅ Takes ownership
✅ Admits failures + lessons
✅ Constructive about past teams
✅ Shows growth

## Part 9: TECHNICAL DEEP DIVE

### Ask About Their Work

Read resume. Pick interesting project.

```
"Tell me about Project X.
- What was your role?
- Why these technical choices?
- What went wrong?
- What would you do differently?
- How does it scale?"
```

### Test Depth

If they say "we used Kafka" — drill:
- Why Kafka over RabbitMQ?
- How configured?
- Issues faced?
- Tuning done?

### Watch for

#### Depth
- Can they go several layers deep?
- Or just surface "we used X"?

#### Curiosity
- Did they investigate why?
- Or just use what was told?

#### Ownership
- Their decisions? Their code?
- Or just followed others?

## Part 11: BIAS AVOIDANCE

### Common Biases

#### Confirmation Bias
> "I think this candidate is great" → ignore evidence to contrary.

**Fix**: Force yourself to find evidence both ways.

#### Affinity Bias
> "Same college as me" → instant approval.

**Fix**: Focus on skills, not background.

#### Halo Effect
> Big company name = good engineer.

**Fix**: Probe actual work, not just title.

#### First Impression
> First 5 min decides → ignore rest.

**Fix**: Keep open mind throughout.

#### Recency Bias
> Last candidate fresh in mind → favored.

**Fix**: Detailed notes immediately.

### Structured Interviews

> Same questions to all candidates. Compare apples to apples.

### Rubrics

> Clear criteria for each level.

```
For L5 Senior Engineer:
- Coding: Solves medium with optimal solution
- Design: Multi-component with trade-offs
- Behavioral: Cross-team influence shown
```

Score against rubric, not gut.

## Part 12: GIVING HINTS

### When to Hint

- Stuck for 5+ min
- Solid approach but missing one piece
- Time running out

### How to Hint

#### Levels of Hint

**Level 1 (Subtle)**: "What if input is sorted?"
**Level 2 (Medium)**: "Think about hash map."
**Level 3 (Direct)**: "Use a hash map to track seen elements."

Start subtle, escalate.

### Don't Hint Too Much

Then you're solving for them. Reduces signal.

### Don't Stay Silent

Stuck candidate getting more stuck = bad. Help nudge.

## Part 13: NOTE-TAKING DURING INTERVIEW

### What to Write

```
Time | Event/Observation
0:05 | Candidate asked good clarifying Qs
0:10 | Discussed 2 approaches, chose better
0:18 | Started coding, slow
0:25 | Off-by-one error, caught it
0:35 | Discussed time complexity correctly
0:40 | Asked good follow-up about scaling
```

### Avoid

- Just "good" or "bad"
- Memory-only
- After-the-fact rationalization

### Right After Interview

- 10 min writing detailed feedback
- Score candidate
- Strong signal: hire/no hire

## Part 14: DEBRIEF MEETING

### Format

```
Each interviewer:
1. Score (Strong Hire / Hire / No Hire / Strong No Hire)
2. 2-3 key strengths observed
3. 2-3 concerns
4. Specific evidence
```

### Discussion

- Calibrate disagreements
- Reference specific moments
- Bar raiser has final say

### Decision

**Hire**: All "hire" or higher
**Borderline**: Discuss, often "no"
**No hire**: Even one "strong no" = no

### Bhai's Rule

> "When in doubt, no hire." Better to miss than hire wrong.

## Part 16: WORK SAMPLES (Take-Home)

### Pros

- Real work scenario
- Time flexibility
- Multiple skills tested

### Cons

- Time intensive
- Can be cheated (AI now)
- Bias toward people with free time

### When Worth It

- Specific role with specific skills
- After phone screen (not first step)
- 4 hours max (respect time)

### Reviewing

- Code quality
- Design choices
- Documentation
- Tests

## Part 21: HIRING WITH AI (2026)

### Reality

- AI writes code
- AI helps interview
- Candidates use AI in take-homes

### Adapt Your Approach

#### Live Coding > Take-Home

Direct observation harder to fake.

#### Verbal Reasoning

"Explain your code." AI can't talk.

#### Architecture Discussion

AI weak at trade-offs.

#### Behavioral

AI can't tell your story.

## Part 22: LEGAL CONSIDERATIONS

### Don'ts

❌ Age, family, religion, citizenship
❌ Salary history (illegal in many places)
❌ Health, disability
❌ Sexual orientation

### Do

✅ "Are you authorized to work in [country]?"
✅ "What are your salary expectations?"
✅ "Any accommodations needed?"

## Part 23: Q&A

### Q: First time interviewing, nervous?
**A**: Shadow experienced interviewer 3-5 times. Then they shadow you.

### Q: Candidate I like but team disagrees?
**A**: Discuss in debrief. Use rubric. Trust the process.

### Q: Asked illegal question by accident?
**A**: Don't use in decision. Apologize. Tell HR.

### Q: Behind on candidate (slow)?
**A**: Note speed concern in feedback. Some are slow but excellent.

### Q: Candidate cheating with AI?
**A**: Direct observation. Verbal reasoning. Ask to share screen.

### Q: When to push back on hire?
**A**: When evidence supports it. Document specifically.

### Q: Disagreeing with senior interviewer?
**A**: Push back respectfully. Bar raiser may decide.
