# 🤝 Cross-Team Collaboration — Senior Guide

> **Target:** 5+ YOE | **Goal:** Multiple teams ke saath kaam karna — influence, communication, dependencies.

---

## Part 1: WHAT — Cross-Team Collaboration Kya?

### Definition

> **Different teams** ke saath joint kaam — shared projects, dependencies, alignment, mutual goals.

### Real-Life Analogy 🏗️

Soch building construction:
- Architecture team
- Civil engineers
- Electrical team
- Plumbing team
- Interior team

Sab alag, sab connected. **Coordination = building.**

## Part 5: BUILDING RELATIONSHIPS

### Step 1: Identify Stakeholders

```
Your project depends on:
- API team
- Data team
- DevOps
- Mobile

Who's the key person each?
```

### Step 2: Reach Out

Don't wait. Introduce yourself:
- Quick coffee/video chat
- "I'm Bhai from X team..."
- "We'll be working together"
- "How can we help each other?"

### Step 3: Establish Trust

- Deliver on commitments
- Help when they need
- Share early
- Communicate often

### Step 4: Maintain

Even when no active project:
- Periodic check-ins
- Share their wins
- Help when asked

## Part 6: DEPENDENCY MANAGEMENT

### Inbound (Others Depend on You)

> They need your team's work.

#### Communicate Early
- Roadmap visibility
- Timeline accuracy
- Changes proactively

#### Set Expectations
- API contracts upfront
- Versioning policy
- Deprecation notices
- Support level

#### Provide Help
- Documentation
- Examples
- Office hours
- Quick responses

### Outbound (You Depend on Others)

> You need their team's work.

#### Communicate Early
- Your needs upfront
- Timeline urgency
- Why important

#### Be Patient
- Other teams have priorities
- Stay realistic
- Don't push too hard

#### Mitigate Risk
- Plan for delays
- Have alternatives
- Build buffer

## Part 9: HANDLING CONFLICTS BETWEEN TEAMS

### Common Conflicts

#### Priority Disagreements
> "Your project is blocking ours."
> "But ours is more important."

#### Approach Differences
> "We should use REST."
> "No, GraphQL is better."

#### Resource Allocation
> "We need more headcount."
> "We do too."

### Resolution Process

#### Step 1: Direct Discussion

Try to resolve at team-to-team level first.

#### Step 2: Document Disagreement

Write down:
- Each side's position
- Constraints
- Trade-offs

#### Step 3: Escalate If Needed

To joint manager or leadership.

#### Step 4: Decide and Commit

Whichever way decided, support it.

## Part 10: WORKING WITH PRODUCT TEAMS

### Product Manager Relationship

> One of most important relationships.

#### Their Perspective
- Customer needs
- Business value
- Market timing
- Stakeholder pressure

#### Your Perspective
- Technical complexity
- Constraints
- Risks
- Time required

### Common Conflicts

#### "Just Add This Feature"
> "Sure, 2 weeks."
> "Why so long? Should be 2 days."

Explain WHY:
- Existing complexity
- Risk
- Dependencies
- Quality requirements

#### "Why Tech Debt Sprint?"
> "Customers need features."

Explain tech debt impact:
- Future velocity
- Bug rate
- Engineering burnout

### Build Trust

- Deliver on commitments
- Share early/often
- Explain trade-offs
- Be transparent

## Part 18: INFLUENCING WITHOUT AUTHORITY

### Tactics

#### 1. Build Coalition
Find allies before proposing.

#### 2. Use Data
"X is better because [data]."

#### 3. Make It About Them
"This helps your team because..."

#### 4. Be Patient
Influence takes time.

#### 5. Pick Battles
Not every hill.

### Anti-Tactics

❌ Going around (escalating prematurely)
❌ Public criticism
❌ Withholding information
❌ Politics over substance

## Part 19: COMMON PITFALLS

### Pitfall 1: Silo Mentality

> "That's their problem, not ours."

Costs whole company.

### Pitfall 2: Local Optimization

> Optimize team's metrics, hurt company.

### Pitfall 3: Surprise Dependencies

> Discover at deadline that other team needed weeks notice.

### Pitfall 4: Throwing Over Wall

> "We're done, your problem now."

### Pitfall 5: Not Knowing Stakeholders

> Build feature, then discover 3 teams affected.

## Part 23: Q&A

### Q: Other team blocking us, what do?
**A**: Direct conversation first. Escalate if no progress.

### Q: My manager wants me focused, but cross-team needed?
**A**: Show impact. Allocate time. Don't sacrifice quality.

### Q: Introvert, hard to network?
**A**: 1-on-1 coffees. Async first. Build slowly.

### Q: Other team's culture toxic?
**A**: Minimize contact. Use official channels. Document.

### Q: Politics tiring?
**A**: Focus on relationships and substance. Politics matter, but secondary.

### Q: Cross-team work seen as wasted time by manager?
**A**: Show business value. Track outcomes.
