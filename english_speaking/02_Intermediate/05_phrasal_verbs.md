# 🔤 Phrasal Verbs — get up, look into, carry out...

Phrasal verbs spoken English ki **reedh ki haddi** hain. Native speakers "investigate" ki jagah "look into", "continue" ki jagah "carry on", "tolerate" ki jagah "put up with" bolte hain. Agar aap formal Latin-wale words bologe to sahi to hoga, par thoda **kitaabi** lagega. Phrasal verbs aapko natural aur fluent banate hain.

---

## Phrasal verb hai kya?

**Phrasal verb = verb + particle (preposition/adverb)**, jinka milke ek naya matlab banta hai jo akele verb se alag hota hai.

- `look` = dekhna → `look into` = **investigate/jaanch karna**
- `give` = dena → `give up` = **haar maan-na**
- `run` = daudna → `run into` = **achanak mil jaana**

> 💡 **Yaad rakho:** Particle add hote hi matlab badal jaata hai. "Turn down a job offer" = offer **thukrana** (volume kam karna nahi!).

### Phrasal verbs kyun zaroori hain?
- Native speakers inhe **har dusre vakya mein** use karte hain.
- Inke bina aapki English samajh to aati hai, par **robot-jaisi/kitaabi** lagti hai.
- Office, debugging, daily life — har jagah ye chhaaye hue hain.

---

## Separable vs Inseparable — bahut important

Kuch phrasal verbs ke beech mein object aa sakta hai (separable), kuch mein nahi (inseparable).

**Separable** — object beech mein aa sakta hai. Aur agar object pronoun ho (it, him, them), to wo **beech mein aana zaroori** hai.

> ✅ "Turn **off** the light." / "Turn the light **off**." (dono sahi)
> ✅ "Turn **it** off." (sahi — pronoun beech mein)
> ❌ "Turn off **it**." (galat!)

**Inseparable** — object hamesha particle ke baad. Beech mein kabhi nahi.

> ✅ "Look **into** the issue." / "Look **into it**."
> ❌ "Look **the issue** into." (galat!)

> 📌 **Rule of thumb:** "look into, run into, come across, get over" jaise **inseparable** hote hain. Confused ho to object ko **end mein** rakho — usually safe.

## Common mistakes

> ❌ "I will look the issue." → ✅ "I will **look into** the issue." (particle zaroori hai)

> ❌ "Please pick up it." → ✅ "Please pick **it** up." (pronoun separable verb ke beech mein)

> ❌ "We need to discuss about it." → ✅ "We need to **discuss it**." ('discuss' phrasal nahi hai, 'about' nahi lagta)

> ❌ "I am working in the login feature." → ✅ "I am working **on** the login feature."

## 🎤 Practice (zor se bolo)

Har sentence loud bolo. Particle par halka zor dena — wahin meaning chhupa hai.

1. "I'll **get back to** you on this by the end of the day."
2. "Can you **look into** this bug before the standup?"
3. "We **came up with** a caching strategy that cut latency in half."
4. "Let me **set up** the development environment first."
5. "The server **went down** at midnight, so we **rolled back** the release."
6. "I need to **catch up on** my emails before the meeting."
7. "We **ran into** a memory leak and finally **tracked down** the cause."
8. "Please **carry on** with the deployment while I review the logs."
9. "She **brought up** a great point, so let's **follow up** on it."
10. "I can't **figure out** why this test keeps failing — let's **work through** it together."
11. "Turn **it** off and **back** it up before you continue." (pronoun placement practice)
12. "We **set out** to reduce errors, and it **turned out** to be a config issue."

**🎯 Mini speaking task:**
Describe karo, English mein, **"how you debug a production issue"** — sirf phrasal verbs use karte hue. Suggested flow: *"First I **look into** the logs, then I try to **narrow down** the problem. If I **run into** something odd, I **track down** the root cause, **figure out** a fix, and **roll out** a patch. If it goes wrong, I **roll back** and **work through** it again."* Bina rukey 45 second bolo.
