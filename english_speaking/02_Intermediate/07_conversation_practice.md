# 💬 Conversation Practice — real dialogues

Grammar aur vocabulary to base hai — par fluency **bolne se** aati hai. Yahan real-life dialogues hain (A aur B lines), jinhe aap **loud, dono roles** bol kar practice karo. Har dialogue ke baad useful phrases di hain. End mein role-play prompts hain.

> 💡 **Sabse important tip:** In dialogues ko sirf padho mat — **bolo**. Pehle A banke bolo, fir B banke. Awaaz record karo aur sun-na. Aap Claude ko mock partner bana sakte ho (neeche bataaya hai).

---

## 1. Greetings & small talk

> **A:** Hey, good morning! How's it going?
> **B:** Morning! Pretty good, thanks. How about you?
> **A:** Can't complain. A little busy, but that's normal these days.
> **B:** Tell me about it. The week's flying by.
> **A:** Right? By the way, how was your weekend?
> **B:** It was relaxing, actually. I just stayed in and caught up on some reading. You?
> **A:** Nice. I went hiking with a few friends — needed the fresh air.
> **B:** That sounds great. Anyway, I should get back to work. Let's catch up later!
> **A:** Sure, talk soon!

**Useful phrases:**
- "How's it going?" / "How are things?" — casual "kaise ho?"
- "Can't complain." — "theek-thaak hi hai" (positive)
- "Tell me about it." — "haan na, bilkul" (agreement)
- "The week's flying by." — "hafta pata hi nahi chal raha"
- "I should get back to work." — "mujhe kaam pe wapas jaana chahiye"
- "Let's catch up later." — "baad mein milte/baat karte hain"

## 2. Introducing yourself (interview / new team)

> **A:** Hi, I'm Ashish. Nice to meet you.
> **B:** Nice to meet you too, Ashish. I'm Priya, the engineering lead here.
> **A:** Great to meet you, Priya. I've heard good things about the team.
> **B:** Thank you! So, tell me a little about yourself.
> **A:** Sure. I'm a backend developer with around six years of experience. I mostly work with Python and have built and scaled several REST APIs and microservices. Lately, I've been focusing on system design and performance optimization.
> **B:** That's impressive. What got you interested in this role?
> **A:** I'm really keen on working at scale, and your team has a strong reputation for solving hard distributed-systems problems. That's exactly the kind of challenge I'm looking for.
> **B:** Wonderful. Let's dive in.

**Useful phrases:**
- "Nice to meet you." → reply: "Nice to meet you too."
- "Tell me a little about yourself." — interview ka classic opener
- "I'm a backend developer with X years of experience." — clean intro line
- "Lately, I've been focusing on..." — recent kaam batane ka natural way
- "What got you interested in this role?" — "is role mein dilchaspi kyun?"
- "That's exactly the kind of challenge I'm looking for." — strong closing line

> ✅ **Tip:** "Myself Ashish" galat hai. Bolo **"I'm Ashish"** ya **"My name is Ashish."**

## 3. At the office — Daily standup (in English)

> **A:** Okay everyone, let's start the standup. Ashish, do you want to go first?
> **B:** Sure. Yesterday, I finished the user-authentication module and wrote the unit tests. Today, I'm going to work on integrating it with the API gateway. No blockers at the moment.
> **A:** Great. Anything you need from anyone?
> **B:** Not right now, but I might need a quick review on my PR later today.
> **A:** Sounds good. Ping me when it's ready and I'll take a look.
> **B:** Will do, thanks.

**Useful phrases (standup formula):**
- **Yesterday:** "Yesterday, I finished/worked on..."
- **Today:** "Today, I'm going to / I'll be working on..."
- **Blockers:** "No blockers." / "I'm blocked on..." / "I'm waiting on..."
- "I might need a quick review on my PR."
- "Ping me when it's ready." — "tayaar ho to bata dena"
- "Will do." — "kar dunga/dungi" (short, natural)

---

## 4. At the office — Asking for help

> **A:** Hey Priya, do you have a minute?
> **B:** Sure, what's up?
> **A:** I'm stuck on a tricky bug. The API returns a 500 error, but only in production — I can't reproduce it locally.
> **B:** Hmm, that's odd. Have you checked the production logs?
> **A:** Yeah, I went through them, but nothing obvious stands out. I was wondering if you could take a quick look with me.
> **B:** Of course. Let's hop on a call and share your screen.
> **A:** That would be a huge help. Thank you so much.
> **B:** No problem at all. Give me five minutes.

**Useful phrases:**
- "Do you have a minute?" — politely time maangna
- "I'm stuck on..." — "main atak gaya hoon..."
- "I can't reproduce it locally." — debugging classic line
- "I was wondering if you could..." — bahut polite request
- "That would be a huge help." — gratitude
- "Let's hop on a call." — "chalo call pe aate hain"

> ❌ "Please do the help." → ✅ "**Could you help me with this?**" / "**Would you mind helping me?**"

## 5. At the office — Giving a status update

> **A:** How's the migration coming along?
> **B:** It's going well. We're about 70% done. The data transfer is complete, and we're now validating the records.
> **A:** Any risks I should know about?
> **B:** One thing — the validation is taking longer than expected. We might need an extra day.
> **A:** Okay, that's fine. Keep me posted, and flag it early if it slips further.
> **B:** Absolutely. I'll send a written update by end of day.

**Useful phrases:**
- "How's it coming along?" — "kaisa chal raha hai?"
- "We're about 70% done." — progress batana
- "It's taking longer than expected." — honest delay update
- "Keep me posted." — "update dete rehna"
- "Flag it early if it slips." — "der ho to pehle hi bata dena"
- "I'll send a written update by end of day." (EOD)

## 6. On a call (phone / video)

> **A:** Hi, can you hear me okay?
> **B:** Yes, loud and clear. Can you hear me?
> **A:** Perfectly. Great. Shall we get started?
> **B:** Sure. Just give me a second to share my screen... Okay, can you see it now?
> **A:** Yes, it's coming through fine.
> **B:** Sorry, you cut out for a second there — could you repeat that?
> **A:** No worries. I was saying, let's start with the architecture overview.
> **B:** Got it. Sounds good.
> *(at the end)*
> **A:** Alright, I think that covers everything. Thanks for your time.
> **B:** Thank you. I'll follow up with the notes. Have a great day!

**Useful phrases (call survival kit):**
- "Can you hear me okay?" / "You're loud and clear."
- "You cut out for a second — could you repeat that?" — "aapki awaaz kat gayi, dobara boliye"
- "Sorry, I didn't catch that." — "main samajh nahi paaya"
- "Shall we get started?" — meeting shuru karna
- "I think that covers everything." — wrap-up
- "I'll follow up with the notes." — "main notes bhej dunga"

> 💡 Call pe awaaz kat-ti hai to ghabrao mat — bas bolo: **"Sorry, you're breaking up. Could you say that again?"**

## 7. Ordering food / shopping

> **A (waiter):** Hi, are you ready to order?
> **B:** Yes. Could I get the grilled chicken with a side of rice, please?
> **A:** Sure. Anything to drink?
> **B:** Just water for now, thanks. Oh, and is the chicken spicy?
> **A:** It's mild, but I can ask the kitchen to make it spicier if you'd like.
> **B:** That would be great, thanks. A little spicy, please.
> **A:** Got it. I'll be right back with your water.
> *(shopping)*
> **B:** Excuse me, do you have this shirt in a medium?
> **A:** Let me check... Yes, here you go.
> **B:** Perfect. How much is it?
> **A:** It's twenty dollars. Would you like to try it on?
> **B:** No, I'll take it. Thank you!

**Useful phrases:**
- "Could I get..., please?" — polite order
- "Anything to drink?" / "Just water for now."
- "Do you have this in a medium?" — size poochhna
- "Would you like to try it on?" / "I'll take it."
- "How much is it?" — "kitne ka hai?"

> ✅ "Could I get..." ya "I'll have..." natural hai. ❌ "I want chicken" thoda rude lagta hai — "**I'd like** the chicken" politer hai.

## 8. Asking for directions

> **A:** Excuse me, could you help me? I'm looking for the central library.
> **B:** Sure! It's not far. Go straight down this road, and take the second left.
> **A:** Second left, okay.
> **B:** Then walk for about two minutes, and it'll be on your right, next to a coffee shop.
> **A:** Got it. Is it within walking distance?
> **B:** Yes, about five minutes on foot.
> **A:** Perfect. Thank you so much for your help!
> **B:** No problem. Have a good day!

**Useful phrases:**
- "Excuse me, I'm looking for..." — direction maangne ka opener
- "Go straight." / "Take the second left." / "It'll be on your right."
- "Is it within walking distance?" — "paidal jaa sakte hain?"
- "About five minutes on foot." — "paidal paanch minute"
- "Thanks for your help!" — hamesha politely close karo

## 9. Making plans

> **A:** Hey, are you free this weekend?
> **B:** I think so. What do you have in mind?
> **A:** I was thinking we could go watch a movie on Saturday. Are you up for it?
> **B:** Sounds good! What time works for you?
> **A:** How about 6 p.m.? We could grab dinner afterwards.
> **B:** Perfect. Let's do that. Shall I book the tickets?
> **A:** Yeah, go ahead. I'll handle dinner.
> **B:** Great, it's a plan!

**Useful phrases:**
- "Are you free this weekend?" — plan poochhna
- "What do you have in mind?" — "kya soch rahe ho?"
- "Are you up for it?" — "mann hai kya?"
- "What time works for you?" — time fix karna
- "Sounds good!" / "It's a plan!" — agreement
- "Let's do that." — "chalo aisa karte hain"

## 10. Casual chat with colleagues

> **A:** Ugh, this bug has been driving me crazy all morning.
> **B:** Oh no, what's going on?
> **A:** A test keeps failing randomly, and I have no idea why.
> **B:** Flaky tests, the worst. Have you tried running it in isolation?
> **A:** Not yet — good call. Let me give that a shot.
> **B:** By the way, are you coming to the team lunch on Friday?
> **A:** Yeah, definitely. I could use a break, honestly.
> **B:** Same here. Alright, I'll let you get back to it. Good luck with the bug!
> **A:** Thanks, I'll need it!

**Useful phrases:**
- "This has been driving me crazy." — "isne mera dimaag kharab kar diya"
- "What's going on?" — "kya ho raha hai?"
- "Good call." — "achhi baat / sahi suggestion"
- "Let me give that a shot." — "main wo try karta hoon"
- "I could use a break." — "ek break ki zaroorat hai"
- "I'll let you get back to it." — "tumhe kaam karne deta hoon"

## 🎤 Practice (zor se bolo) — Role-play prompts

In dialogues ko sirf padho mat — **role-play karo**. Dono roles loud bolo, ya Claude ke saath karo.

1. **Standup karo:** Apna real kal-aaj-blocker English mein bolo, standup formula use karke. ("Yesterday I... Today I'll... No blockers.")
2. **Apna intro do:** 60 seconds mein "tell me about yourself" ka jawab bolo, bina ruke.
3. **Help maango:** Ek real bug pick karo aur politely help maango: "I'm stuck on... I was wondering if you could..."
4. **Status update do:** "How's it coming along?" ka jawab do — progress %, ek risk, aur "I'll keep you posted."
5. **Call simulate karo:** "Can you hear me okay?" se shuru karke "Thanks for your time" tak full call bolo, beech mein "you cut out, could you repeat?" daalo.
6. **Khana order karo:** Restaurant scene bolo — order, ek sawaal (spicy hai?), aur bill maango.
7. **Direction poochho:** Kisi se nearest metro station ka raasta poochho aur "is it within walking distance?" add karo.
8. **Plan banao:** Ek dost ke saath weekend movie plan fix karo — time, dinner, tickets.
9. **Casual chat:** Ek colleague ke saath "is bug ne pareshan kar diya" wali casual baat karo.
10. **Small talk:** "How's it going?" se shuru kar ke 5 lines ka natural small talk bolo.

**🎯 Mini speaking task — Claude ke saath mock practice:**
Aap ye dialogues **Claude ke saath live practice** kar sakte ho. Bas Claude ko bolo:

> *"Be my mock interviewer. Ask me 'tell me about yourself' and then ask 3 follow-up questions. Correct my English after each answer."*

ya

> *"Let's role-play a daily standup. You're my team lead. Start by asking me for my update."*

ya

> *"Pretend you're a waiter at a restaurant. Take my order in English and keep the conversation going."*

Roz **ek role-play** karo. 30 din mein aapki bolne ki jhijhak khatam ho jaayegi. Bolne se hi aati hai — padhne se nahi!
