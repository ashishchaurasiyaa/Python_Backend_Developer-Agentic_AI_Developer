# 🧩 Intermediate Grammar — prepositions, modals, conjunctions, questions

Spoken English mein chhoti galtiyan — galat preposition, galat modal — turant pakdi jaati hain. Yeh chapter un chhote-chhote "glue words" ko sahi karta hai jo har sentence mein aate hain.

---

## 📍 Part 1 — Prepositions of Time (in / on / at)

Yeh teen sabse zyada confuse karte hain. Rule simple hai:

| Preposition | Use for (Hinglish) | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **in** | bade time periods — months, years, seasons, parts of day | in June, in 2024, in summer, in the morning |
| **on** | specific days aur dates | on Monday, on 15th August, on my birthday |
| **at** | exact/point time, festivals | at 5 PM, at noon, at night, at the weekend (UK) |

**Easy mental model:** `at` = point (chhota), `on` = surface/day (medium), `in` = container/period (bada). Zoom out karte jao: **at** 6 → **on** Monday → **in** July.

**Examples:**
- The meeting is **at** 3 PM **on** Monday **in** December.
- I was born **in** 1995.
- See you **in** the morning, but **at** night I'm offline.

> ❌ Common mistake: "I will meet you **in** Monday." / "The standup is **in** 10 AM."
> ✅ Correct: "I will meet you **on** Monday." / "The standup is **at** 10 AM."

> ❌ Common mistake (very Indian): "I will revert **on** the same." / "Please discuss **on** this."
> ✅ Correct: "I will **get back to you** on this." / "Please discuss **this**." (discuss ke baad "on/about" nahi lagta — discuss is already complete.)

**No preposition with:** today, tomorrow, yesterday, this/last/next + time.
- ✅ "I'll send it **tomorrow**." (NOT "on tomorrow")
- ✅ "We met **last week**." (NOT "in last week")

## 🗺️ Part 2 — Prepositions of Place (in / on / at)

| Preposition | Use for | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **in** | enclosed/inside, cities, countries | in the room, in Pune, in India, in the car |
| **on** | surface, floors, streets | on the table, on the 3rd floor, on the wall, on the bus |
| **at** | specific point/place, addresses | at the door, at the office, at the bus stop, at home |

**Examples:**
- He is **at** the office, sitting **in** a meeting room, **on** the 4th floor.
- The file is **on** the desktop, **in** the Downloads folder.

> 💡 Transport ka mazedaar exception: **in** a car/taxi, but **on** a bus/train/plane/bike. (chhoti cheez = in, badi cheez jisme chal sakte ho = on.)

## 🏃 Part 3 — Prepositions of Movement

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **to** | direction/destination | I'm going **to** the office. |
| **into** | bahar se andar | He walked **into** the room. |
| **out of** | andar se bahar | She came **out of** the meeting. |
| **onto** | upar surface pe | The cat jumped **onto** the table. |
| **through** | beech mein se paar | We drove **through** the tunnel. |
| **across** | ek taraf se doosri | He ran **across** the road. |
| **towards** | ki taraf | She walked **towards** me. |
| **from** | source/origin | The bug came **from** the cache layer. |

> ❌ Common mistake: "I am going **to** home." / "He returned back **to** office."
> ✅ Correct: "I am going **home**." (home ke saath "to" nahi.) / "He returned **to** the office." (returned mein "back" already implied hai — "returned back" double ho gaya.)

## 🔑 Part 4 — Modal Verbs

Modals = helper verbs jo ability, permission, possibility, obligation, advice dikhate hain. **Hamesha V1 (base verb) lagao** modal ke baad — koi -s, koi -ed nahi.

| Modal | Meaning (Hinglish) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **can** | ability / informal permission | I **can** fix this. · **Can** I leave early? |
| **could** | past ability / polite request / possibility | I **could** code in C as a kid. · **Could** you help me? · It **could** rain. |
| **may** | formal permission / possibility | **May** I come in? · It **may** be a network issue. |
| **might** | weaker possibility (less sure than may) | The deploy **might** fail. |
| **should** | advice / recommendation / mild expectation | You **should** add tests. · It **should** work now. |
| **would** | polite request / hypothetical / past habit | **Would** you mind reviewing? · I **would** do it differently. |
| **must** | strong obligation (self/internal) / strong certainty | I **must** finish this today. · He **must** be busy. |
| **have to** | external obligation (rule/boss) | I **have to** attend the standup. |
| **must not** | prohibition (mana hai) | You **must not** push to main directly. |
| **don't have to** | optional (zaroori nahi) | You **don't have to** come early. |

**Deep dive — sabse zaroori distinctions:**

**1) can vs could vs may (permission/request):** politeness badhti jaati hai →
- "**Can** you send it?" (casual, dosto/team mein)
- "**Could** you send it?" (polite, professional — interview/manager ke saath best)
- "**May** I send it later?" (most formal)

**2) must vs have to (obligation):**
- **must** = andar se / khud decide kiya → "I **must** improve my English." (internal)
- **have to** = bahar se rule → "I **have to** submit the timesheet by Friday." (company rule)

**3) must not vs don't have to (BIG confusion):**
- **must not** = strictly mana → "You **must not** share the password." (prohibited)
- **don't have to** = optional, marzi → "You **don't have to** stay late." (allowed to leave)

> ❌ Common mistake: "I **should to** review it." / "She **can** to help." / "He **must** finishes it."
> ✅ Correct: "I **should review** it." / "She **can help**." / "He **must finish** it." — modal ke baad seedha base verb, na "to", na "-s/-es".

> ❌ Common mistake: "**Can** you tell me the time?" — galat nahi, par interview mein **could** zyada polite lagta hai.
> ✅ Better: "**Could** you tell me the time?"

## 🔗 Part 5 — Conjunctions (joining words)

| Conjunction | Use (Hinglish) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **and** | jodne ke liye | I wrote the code **and** ran the tests. |
| **but** | contrast / lekin | It compiles, **but** the tests fail. |
| **or** | choice / ya | We can deploy now **or** wait till evening. |
| **so** | result / isliye | The API was slow, **so** I added caching. |
| **because** | reason / kyunki | I'm late **because** the build broke. |
| **although / though** | despite / haalanki | **Although** it's risky, let's try it. |
| **while** | same time / contrast | **While** I deploy, you watch the logs. |

> ❌ Common mistake: "**Because** the build broke, **so** I'm late." (because aur so dono ek saath nahi.)
> ✅ Correct: "I'm late **because** the build broke." OR "The build broke, **so** I'm late." — ek hi use karo.

> ❌ Common mistake: "**Although** it's risky, **but** let's try." (although + but redundant.)
> ✅ Correct: "**Although** it's risky, let's try." OR "It's risky, **but** let's try."

## ❓ Part 6 — Question Formation

### Yes/No questions
Structure: `Auxiliary + subject + main verb?`
- "**Do** you work here?" · "**Did** he call?" · "**Are** you coming?" · "**Have** you finished?"

### Wh- questions
Structure: `Wh-word + auxiliary + subject + verb?`

| Wh-word | Puchhta hai | Example |
|---|---|---|
| What | cheez | **What** are you working on? |
| Where | jagah | **Where** is the config file? |
| When | time | **When** did you deploy? |
| Why | reason | **Why** did the test fail? |
| Who | person | **Who** wrote this function? |
| Which | choice | **Which** branch are you on? |
| How | manner/degree | **How** does this work? · **How** long will it take? |

> ❌ Common mistake (very common): "**What** you are doing?" / "**Where** he is going?" (word order galat.)
> ✅ Correct: "**What are you** doing?" / "**Where is he** going?" — wh-word ke baad **auxiliary phir subject**.

> ❌ Common mistake: "**Why** you didn't call?" 
> ✅ Correct: "**Why didn't you** call?"

### Question Tags (na? / hai na?)
Statement + chhota ulta tag. Positive statement → negative tag, negative statement → positive tag.

| Statement | Tag | Full |
|---|---|---|
| You're a developer, | aren't you? | "You're a developer, **aren't you?**" |
| He didn't push it, | did he? | "He didn't push it, **did he?**" |
| We can deploy now, | can't we? | "We can deploy now, **can't we?**" |

> ❌ Common mistake (Indian English ka famous error): "You did it, **isn't it?**" / "He is coming, **no?**"
> ✅ Correct: "You did it, **didn't you?**" / "He is coming, **isn't he?**" — tag ko statement ke verb se match karao, "isn't it" sabke liye nahi chalta.

### Indirect questions (politeness ke liye — interview gold)
Direct question ko ek polite phrase mein wrap karo. Andar **word order normal** ho jaata hai (auxiliary front pe nahi).

| Direct (blunt) | Indirect (polite) |
|---|---|
| Where is the meeting room? | **Could you tell me where** the meeting room **is**? |
| What does this API do? | **Do you know what** this API **does**? |
| When will it be ready? | **May I ask when** it **will be** ready? |

> ❌ Common mistake: "Could you tell me **where is** the room?" (indirect mein verb ulta mat karo.)
> ✅ Correct: "Could you tell me **where the room is**?"

## 🔢 Part 7 — Countable vs Uncountable Nouns

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Countable** | gin sakte ho (1, 2, 3...) | bug, file, server, idea, task |
| **Uncountable** | gin nahi sakte (mass/abstract) | water, information, advice, work, software, feedback, knowledge, traffic |

**Quantifier rules:**

| Word | Countable | Uncountable | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **many** | ✅ | ❌ | "**many** bugs" |
| **much** | ❌ | ✅ | "**much** information" |
| **few / a few** | ✅ | ❌ | "**a few** tasks" |
| **little / a little** | ❌ | ✅ | "**a little** time" |
| **some** | ✅ | ✅ | "**some** files / **some** water" |
| **any** | ✅ | ✅ | "**any** questions / **any** feedback" |

> 💡 some vs any: **some** = positive sentences aur polite offers/requests. **any** = negatives aur questions.
> - "I have **some** doubts." · "Would you like **some** coffee?" (offer)
> - "I don't have **any** doubts." · "Do you have **any** questions?"

> ❌ Common mistake (super Indian): "I have many **works** today." / "Give me some **informations**." / "He gave me good **advices**."
> ✅ Correct: "I have **a lot of work** today." / "Give me some **information**." / "He gave me good **advice**." — work, information, advice, software, feedback, equipment, news — **kabhi plural nahi**, aur "a" nahi lagta.

> ❌ Common mistake: "Today I have **less** meetings." 
> ✅ Correct: "Today I have **fewer** meetings." (countable → fewer; uncountable → less, jaise "less work".)

## 🎤 Practice (zor se bolo)

Zor se bolo, fir apne examples banao:

1. "The meeting is **at** 10 AM **on** Monday **in** January." (prepositions of time)
2. "The config file is **in** the `src` folder, **on** the second line." (place)
3. "He walked **into** the room and came **out of** the meeting early." (movement)
4. "**Could** you please review my pull request?" (polite modal)
5. "You **must not** push directly to main, but you **don't have to** wait for me." (must not vs don't have to)
6. "It compiles, **but** the integration tests fail." (conjunction — contrast)
7. "I added caching **because** the API was slow." (reason)
8. "**Where is** the config file?" — sahi word order bolo. (wh-question)
9. "You deployed it, **didn't you?**" (question tag — NOT "isn't it")
10. "**Could you tell me where** the build logs **are**?" (indirect/polite question)
11. "I have **a lot of work** and only **a little** time today." (uncountable)
12. "We have **fewer** meetings but **much more** focus this week." (fewer/much)

**Fix these out loud:**
- ❌ "Why you didn't call?" → ✅ ?
- ❌ "He gave me good advices." → ✅ ?
- ❌ "You did it, isn't it?" → ✅ ?

**🗣️ Mini speaking task:** Apne manager ke saath ek 1-minute imaginary conversation bolo jisme tum: (a) ek polite indirect question pucho, (b) ek modal se permission lo ("Could I...?"), (c) ek reason do "because" se, aur (d) ek question tag use karo. Record karke suno — kya prepositions natural lag rahe hain?
