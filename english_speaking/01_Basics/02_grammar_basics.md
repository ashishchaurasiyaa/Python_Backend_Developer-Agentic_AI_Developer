# 📐 Grammar Basics — is/am/are, has/have, do/does, a/an/the

Ye lesson un chhoti words ke baare mein hai jo har English sentence mein aate hain — aur jahan Indian speakers sabse zyada galti karte hain. Inhe pakka kar lo to aadhi English theek ho jaati hai.

## 🟢 Be-verbs: am / is / are

"Be-verb" wo word hai jo subject ke saath jodta hai — koi cheez "hai" batane ke liye. Hindi mein hum bolte hain "hoon / hai / ho / hain", English mein **am / is / are**.

| Subject | Be-verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | am | I **am** a developer. |
| He / She / It | is | She **is** my manager. |
| You / We / They | are | They **are** in a meeting. |

Rule yaad rakhne ka trick: **I → am**, **single cheez (he/she/it) → is**, **multiple ya you/we → are**.

> ❌ Common mistake: "I is busy." / "They is ready." / "He are late."
> ✅ Correct: "I **am** busy." / "They **are** ready." / "He **is** late."

### Be-verb ka sabse bada Indian trap: "having" galat jagah

Hindi mein "mere paas car hai" ko log direct "I am having a car" bol dete hain. Ye GALAT hai. Possession (kisi cheez ka hona) ke liye **have/has** use karo, "am having" nahi.

> ❌ Common mistake: "I am having a car." / "I am having two brothers." / "She is having a laptop."
> ✅ Correct: "I **have** a car." / "I **have** two brothers." / "She **has** a laptop."

("Having" sirf tab sahi hai jab matlab ho khaana/experience — "I am having lunch", "We are having a meeting".)

## 🟡 have / has — possession aur relationships

"have/has" matlab "paas hona / rakhna".

| Subject | have/has | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I / You / We / They | have | I **have** five years of experience. |
| He / She / It | has | She **has** a strong backend background. |

> ❌ Common mistake: "He have a meeting." / "She have two laptops."
> ✅ Correct: "He **has** a meeting." / "She **has** two laptops." — he/she/it ke saath **has**.

## 🔵 do / does — questions aur negatives banane ke liye

Action verbs (work, write, go, like) ke saath jab sawaal ya negative banana ho, tab **do/does** lagta hai.

| Subject | helper | Example question | Example negative |
|---|---|---|---|
| I / You / We / They | do | **Do** you work here? | I **do not** (don't) work here. |
| He / She / It | does | **Does** she write code? | He **does not** (doesn't) write code. |

### Bahut important rule: does/doesn't ke baad verb mein "s" nahi lagta

Jab "does" aa gaya, to main verb apne base form mein rehta hai (no -s).

> ❌ Common mistake: "Does she writes code?" / "He doesn't works here."
> ✅ Correct: "Does she **write** code?" / "He doesn't **work** here." — "does" ne -s pehle hi le liya, ab verb plain.

> ❌ Common mistake: "What you do?" / "Where she works?" (helper missing)
> ✅ Correct: "What **do** you do?" / "Where **does** she work?"

## 🟣 Articles: a / an / the — Indians ki classic galti

Hindi mein "a/an/the" jaisa kuch nahi hota, isliye ye sabse mushkil lagta hai. Dheere-dheere pakka hoga.

### a vs an
- **a** — consonant sound se shuru hone waale word se pehle: *a developer, a laptop, a project.*
- **an** — vowel SOUND (a, e, i, o, u) se shuru hone waale word se pehle: *an engineer, an API, an hour.*

Dhyaan: SOUND maayne rakhta hai, spelling nahi. "an hour" (h silent hai, sound vowel), par "a university" ("you" sound se shuru, isliye a).

| Word | Article | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| engineer | **an** engineer | vowel sound |
| laptop | **a** laptop | consonant sound |
| API | **an** API ("ay-pee-eye") | vowel sound |
| hour | **an** hour | h silent |
| university | **a** university | "you" sound |

### a/an vs the
- **a/an** = koi bhi ek (general, pehli baar mention): "I need **a** laptop."
- **the** = wahi specific waala (dono ko pata hai kaunsa): "Please pass **the** laptop on the table."

| Sentence | Kyun |
|---|---|
| I joined **a** company last year. | pehli baar, koi ek company |
| **The** company is hiring now. | ab wahi specific company |

> ❌ Common mistake: "I am developer." / "She is manager." (article missing — sabse aam Indian galti)
> ✅ Correct: "I am **a** developer." / "She is **a** manager." — kisi profession/single noun se pehle article zaroori.

> ❌ Common mistake: "I am going to office." / "He is in hospital." (Indian habit)
> ✅ Correct (general): "I am going to **the** office." / "He is in **the** hospital." (kuch fixed phrases jaise "go to school/work/home" article nahi lete — woh exceptions yaad rakho.)

## 🟠 Present Simple Tense — basics

Roz ki aadat, fact, ya routine batane ke liye present simple use hota hai.

| Subject | Verb form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I / You / We / They | base form | I **work** from home. |
| He / She / It | base + **s/es** | She **works** from home. |

Rule: **he/she/it** ke saath verb mein **-s** lagta hai. Baaki sab ke saath plain verb.

| Subject | go | watch | study |
|---|---|---|---|
| I / We / They | go | watch | study |
| He / She | **goes** | **watches** | **studies** |

## 🤝 Subject–Verb Agreement (he goes, they go)

Verb ko subject ke hisaab se match karna hi "agreement" hai. Single person (he/she/it) → verb mein -s. Plural ya I/you/we/they → plain verb.

| Correct | Wrong |
|---|---|
| He **goes** to the gym. | ~~He go to the gym.~~ |
| They **go** to the gym. | ~~They goes to the gym.~~ |
| She **watches** videos. | ~~She watch videos.~~ |
| We **watch** videos. | ~~We watches videos.~~ |

> ❌ Common mistake: "He go to office" / "They goes home" / "My friend live in Delhi."
> ✅ Correct: "He **goes** to the office." / "They **go** home." / "My friend **lives** in Delhi."

Ek line yaad rakho: **"He, she, it — verb mein s lagaa de. I, you, we, they — verb plain rehne de."**

## ✅ Quick recap

- am/is/are → "hona". I→am, single→is, plural/you/we→are.
- have/has → "paas hona". he/she/it→has, baaki→have. Possession ke liye "having" mat bolo.
- do/does → questions/negatives banane ka helper. does ke baad verb plain.
- a/an/the → article. Sound dekho (a/an), specific cheez ke liye the. Profession se pehle article lagao.
- Present simple: he/she/it ke saath verb mein -s.

## 🎤 Practice (zor se bolo)

Har sentence saaf, zor se bolo. Underline waale word pe dhyaan do.

1. I **am** a backend developer.
2. She **is** my team lead, and they **are** in a meeting.
3. I **have** five years of experience.
4. He **has** a strong understanding of databases.
5. **Do** you work in the payments team?
6. **Does** she review the pull requests?
7. He **does not** work here anymore.
8. I am **an** engineer at **a** product company.
9. She **goes** to the office, but they **work** from home.
10. My friend **lives** in Bangalore and **writes** Java.

Fix karo (galti pakdo aur sahi bolo):
11. "He have a interview today." → *He **has an** interview today.*
12. "I am having a car and she go to office." → *I **have** a car and she **goes** to the office.*
